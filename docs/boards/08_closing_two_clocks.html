Two ways to be together

When Pope Francis died, the world searched for him in the same hour: 2:20 a.m. in Mexico, 5 p.m. in Japan, nearly 8 p.m. in New Zealand. That is one way to share a moment. The weather is the other.

Shocks line up on the world’s clock. So do appointments: a final kicks off at one moment for everyone, even where it is 3 a.m. Rituals line up on each country’s own clock: the weather at 6 a.m., the countdown at midnight. Waves do a little of both, coming back for months, sometimes slowly, sometimes all at once.

The rule we used to find the moments the world shared was built for the world’s clock: 15 countries within an hour. In 18 months it caught the weather only once, on a May morning when 13 European countries woke up in the same hour. On a usual day, about 20 countries search the weather at 6 a.m. their time. The most repeated search in the archive was almost invisible to it.

So what did your country share with the world, and what did it keep to itself?

The world keeps a fixture list

Shocks are rare. Most of the time, when the world searches for the same thing, it is because it agreed to in advance. Nine in ten global moments were scheduled, 7,236 of 7,999, and 98 percent of those were sport.

On 481 of 486 days, at least one scheduled event trended in 15 or more countries. On a typical day there were 13.

They pile up on weekends: 24 on an average Saturday, 7 on a Monday.

On 204 days, one of them reached more than 100 countries. 199 of those were football matches. The news did that 15 times in 18 months.

The scheduled events you might expect, the Oscars, an inauguration, the conclave, are the exceptions on this calendar.

Color by: widest reach
The fairer measure (see the caveat): how far the day's widest event reached, not how many events there were. Weekends (Saturdays averaged 103 countries, Mondays 68) and Champions League nights still stand out.

A caveat. Google's Trending Now is built to catch sudden jumps. A football match is a new search every time two teams meet, the kind of jump the system is designed to see, while an award show or an election builds over days. So this data may make football look even more dominant than it is; we compare reach, not counts, to limit that.
▸ How we measured this
Days in UTC. Hatched days had gaps in Google's collection. Each event counted once, on the day of its scheduled start.
The comparison
A regular Spanish league match reached more countries than the Oscars
State 1 · guess first
Real Madrid vs Rayo Vallecano, a league match on a Sunday in February, trended in 123 countries. How many searched the Oscars? Drag to guess.
State 2 · reveal
The Oscars reached 81. Outside sport, only the conclave (115) and the Grammys (102) passed 100. And not every match is huge: a typical one reached 35 countries.
Event reach: countries where any topic about the event trended in its window. League matches identified from their searches (a lower bound). Non-sport events: catalogue and hand-verified events only; single-topic units (e.g. “Samsung”, “BTS”) left out.

All at Once

Most of the time, every country is searching for something different. Every so often, the whole world types the same thing within minutes. What makes that happen?

By Belen Reyes  ·  Dec. 10, 2026

At 7:50 a.m. Greenwich time on April 21, 2025, the Vatican announced that Pope Francis had died. When Google next refreshed its lists of what was trending, he was already on them in 20 countries, from Hong Kong to Peru.

Ten minutes later it was 60. Within the hour, 109. People who did not share a language, a time zone or, in many cases, a religion were asking the same question in the same few minutes.

Moments like this are rare, and they are not all alike. Reading 18 months of trending searches, we found that the world looks up together in four ways: shocks nobody saw coming, appointments everybody had in the calendar, waves that keep coming back, and small rituals repeated every day.

# Methodology

How the numbers in the article were produced, in plain language. Every figure here is taken from [`STORY_NUMBERS.md`](STORY_NUMBERS.md) (the article's numbers, regenerated by the pipeline), [`DECISIONS.md`](DECISIONS.md) (every choice and threshold), [`PROFILE.md`](PROFILE.md) or [`CHECKS.md`](CHECKS.md). If a number here and in those files ever differ, those files are right.

## Data source

- **Dataset:** *Google Trend Archive: Global Real-Time Search Trends*, by Aleksandra Urman, Anikó Hannák and Joachim Baumann (ICWSM 2026), on Hugging Face as [`aurman/GoogleTrendArchive`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/aurman/GoogleTrendArchive), https://doi.org/10.57967/hf/7531, licensed CC BY 4.0. We use its `trending_queries` file (`googletrendarchive_preprocessed_170526.csv`) and its location list (DECISIONS.md, Step 1).
- **What it is:** the authors archived Google's "Trending now" lists for every available country and many sub-national regions.
- **Coverage:** 10,204,597 trend rows. Trend start times run from 2024-11-27 23:00 UTC to 2026-05-17 10:20 UTC (PROFILE.md).
- **What we use:** the **125 national feeds**, one per country.
- **Collection interval:** about 10 minutes. Google says the Trending Now data is "refreshed on average every ten minutes". In the archive, 100% of trend start times fall on a 10-minute grid (CHECKS.md, C4).

## What "trending" means

Google's [Trending Now](https://support.google.com/trends/answer/3076011) lists are **not the most-searched terms**.
- **A sudden rise:** they show searches whose interest has risen sharply compared with what Google expected for that country (Google reports volume as growth against a "predicted baseline").
- **Grouped queries:** each entry can stand for a group of related queries or variants that Google clusters together.
- **No published thresholds:** Google doesn't publish the size of rise needed, the volume cutoff, or how it forms the groups.

So a topic "trends" in a country when people there suddenly search for it much more than usual. A permanently popular search (YouTube, the weather) trends only when interest in it spikes.

## Our processing

Each step is a script in [`pipeline/`](../pipeline); `make` targets run them in order (see the README).

1. **Download** (`01_download.py`). The dataset files are fetched from Hugging Face.
2. **Cleaning** (`02_clean.py`):
   - **Times:** the archive stores times labeled as UTC that are really **Europe/Berlin wall-clock time**, so we read them as Berlin time and convert to UTC. Three known events (Pope Francis's death, the Cloudflare outage, Jimmy Carter's death) line up within 0 minutes of their real times (DECISIONS.md, Step 2).
   - **Locations:** we keep country and region rows but drop numeric US metro codes (1,127,988 rows).
   - **National feeds only:** every count in the article uses the 125 national feeds; regional feeds are used only for time-of-day statistics in the US, Canada and Russia, which span several time zones.
3. **Topic merging** (`03_topics.py`, with `combine.py`, `recall.py` and `link.py`). Different spellings, word orders and languages of the same thing are merged into one **topic**. Searches are first normalized (case, accents, punctuation). Then several kinds of evidence join them:
   - **Typos** within a 3-day window.
   - **Embedding similarity**, backed by Google's own related searches.
   - **Same English name:** an English name and type that Claude (Anthropic's language model) gave each important search.
   - **Wikidata IDs.**
   - **Word-order and plural variants,** and **sports-match keys** built from the two teams' names in any language.

   Guards stop bad merges. Two different Wikidata IDs never merge. Short or single-word strings ("real", "cf") join a topic only with strong evidence. Groups are capped in size. Generic event names ("Earthquake") merge only within a shared country. A small hand-made override list handles a few remaining cases.

   The result: 895,618 distinct searches become 765,159 topics (DECISIONS.md, "Open issues").

   **Validation:** pairs of searches were labeled by hand as same or different topic. On a fresh sample drawn after all merge rules, weighted to represent all pairs the pipeline could merge, **precision is about 0.97 and recall about 0.64** (DECISIONS.md, "Final validation results"). When the pipeline merges two searches it is almost always right, but it still keeps apart about a third of the pairs that belong together, mostly cross-language paraphrases.
4. **Moments** (`04_moments.py`).
   - **Moment:** one topic in one burst of attention. In each country, a topic's trends form one session until the country goes 24 hours without it. A new moment starts when the next session starts more than 6 hours after the previous one.
   - **Global moment:** a moment in which **at least 15 countries had started trending the topic within 60 minutes of the third country's first trend**. Measuring from the third country keeps one or two early outliers from hiding a real global spike.
   - **Sport:** each moment carries `is_sport`. That's matches and teams in Claude's labels, plus leagues and competitions by name; athletes are not counted.
   - **How many:** 8,014 global moments in total (STORY_NUMBERS.md, "Time machine").
5. **Kinds** (`08_story_export.py`, the same rules as [`STORY_CANDIDATES.md`](STORY_CANDIDATES.md)). Each topic gets one of five kinds, by rule, from Claude's type and its timing:
   - **ritual:** weather, holidays, lotteries, prayer times, daily puzzles and prices, or a product or company that keeps coming back (15+ separate moments of 10+ countries, outside sport);
   - **appointment:** scheduled in advance: sport, finals, awards, elections, releases, launches, draws, and people who trend in 10+ separate moments (athletes, performers);
   - **shock:** sudden and unscheduled. Half of its countries are reached within 3 hours, in 20+ countries, with at most 4 days in 10+ countries. Mostly deaths and outages;
   - **wave:** spreads more slowly or lasts longer (AI products, long news stories);
   - **local:** its biggest moment reached fewer than 15 countries (unless it's a ritual).

   These are reading aids, not ground truth. The sub-labels for shocks (death, outage, disaster, politics, …) are keyword rules too, and some are wrong; STORY_NUMBERS.md lists the biggest so they can be checked.
6. **Checks** (`07_checks.py` → [`CHECKS.md`](CHECKS.md)): known events, time zones, collection artifacts, sensitivity to thresholds, collection gaps, and the integrity of the exported files.
7. **Story export** (`08_story_export.py` → `data/export/story/` and [`STORY_NUMBERS.md`](STORY_NUMBERS.md)): one data file per chapter, plus every number the article quotes with the file and key it comes from.

## Limitations

- **Every country counts once.** "120 countries" weighs Iceland and India the same. Reach measures how widely something spread, not how many people searched.
- **10-minute resolution.** Times are known only to the nearest collection pass, so many countries "start" at exactly the same minute. For example, 20 countries were already trending Pope Francis at the first pass after the announcement (STORY_NUMBERS.md, "Opening"). Differences of a few minutes don't mean anything. Separately, the collector shows an excess of trends starting in the 19:00 UTC hour, which shifts "busiest hour" figures (CHECKS.md, C4). We flag those trends but don't change them.
- **Collection gaps.** The archive has **486 days with full collection coverage out of 537** (STORY_NUMBERS.md, "Rituals"). Days are missing in 2024-11 (1 day), 2025-09 (19 days), 2025-12 (4 days) and 2026-01 (27 days) (STORY_NUMBERS.md, header), so monthly series dip in those months.
- **Who is missing.** Only countries where Google publishes Trending Now are in the data. **China** and **North Korea** are absent, and in countries where few people use Google, the feed shows the habits of Google's users, not the whole population. Five countries have so few trends per day that their Wrapped cards are flagged `low_data` (STORY_NUMBERS.md, "Wrapped").
- **Google shapes what trends.** Trending Now depends on Google's undisclosed baselines, thresholds and grouping, and on how people use Google's products. Features such as live match cards and score boxes may make some kinds of searches (sport fixtures, for example) more likely to surge and be listed. We can't separate that effect from genuine interest.
- **Machine labels.** Topic names, types and kinds come from Claude's labels and from rules. They are checked by hand samples and spot reviews, but not every one was reviewed.
- **Sensitive topics we hid.** Some local trends name private individuals in accidents, crimes or other distressing stories. A topic is hidden from all published lists if it matches crime, death, sexual or accident keywords and trended in **2 countries or fewer**. That's 3,839 topics in total (STORY_NUMBERS.md, header). The highest-priority sensitive topics shown on the site were reviewed by hand, and all of them were kept. Hidden topics still count in totals and statistics; they just never appear by name. Wrapped cards also skip accident, crash, shooting and death events. Hand-review files that may contain private names are kept out of this repository (see [`REVIEW_FILES.md`](REVIEW_FILES.md)).

## Reproducing

From the project root, after `make setup`:

```bash
make download clean-data   # steps 1-2
make topics                # step 3: strings ... combine, link, validation (Claude and Wikidata results are read from cache/)
make after-topics          # steps 4-7: moments, export, profile, checks
make story                 # step 8: data/export/story/ and docs/STORY_NUMBERS.md
```

- **Caches:** the Claude labels and the Wikidata lookups were run once and are cached in `cache/` (git-ignored). `make topics` reads them and makes no paid API call. Rebuilding them from scratch needs an Anthropic API key and the `claude-*` and `wikidata-*` stages of `03_topics.py` (README and DECISIONS.md).
- **Hand decisions:** sensitive-topic decisions are read from `pipeline/sensitive_decisions.csv`, which is kept local. Without it, every topic matching the default rule is hidden.
- **What to cite:** `docs/STORY_NUMBERS.md` is regenerated on every `make story`. Cite numbers from it, not from this page.
